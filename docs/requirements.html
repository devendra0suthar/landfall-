Landfall

A worldwide job-application platform. Upload a résumé once; Landfall finds matching roles anywhere on earth, prepares each application from your own facts, and files it wherever it is allowed to — recording exactly what was sent.

01

Why this, and why now

Applying for work has become data entry. The median application form asks about fifteen questions — the same nine of which cover half of every form on the market — and a serious search means answering them two hundred times.

Candidates outside the US pay an extra tax. The roles are global, the forms are American, and the regional boards that actually hold the jobs — Naukri in India, Seek and JobStreet across APAC, Xing in the German-speaking market — are invisible to most tools built in San Francisco.

A crowded field already exists. Most of it solves the typing and creates a worse problem: it writes the candidate's claims for them.

The wedge

Everyone competes on volume. Nobody competes on honesty, or on evidence of what actually happened. Landfall takes the opposite bet.

Greenhouse can be planned offline and then executed as a known plan. 03

Principles

Carried over from Phase 0. These are product decisions, not engineering preferences — they are the whole difference between Landfall and the table above.

01

The candidate's words, or nothing

Tailoring selects and orders the bullets they wrote. It never rewrites one and never invents one. Every document passes a verbatim integrity check before it can be sent.

02

Attestations are never automated

"I certify this is true", privacy notices, EEO and demographic questions, visa and health declarations always reach the human. Ticking one on someone's behalf is an attestation they did not make. It costs about 1% of fields.

03

Bot walls are recorded, never evaded

A CAPTCHA, rate limit or reputation check ends the run and hands the session back. No solving services, no residential proxy rotation, no fingerprint spoofing.

04

Submission is allowlisted; reading is not

A destination becomes submittable by explicit configuration. Never by default, never by inference from "it worked in testing".

05

Every send is archived

The exact bytes that went to the employer are stored and hashed. Three weeks later, at the moment an interview makes it matter, "which CV did they see?" has an answer.

06

No claimed success without evidence

An application is submitted, not confirmed, until a confirmation email or a status poll says otherwise.

05

Scope

v0 — prove the engine

Greenhouse only · ~8 weeks
  • Résumé upload, parsed into a correctable structured profile
  • Discovery across Greenhouse boards on a live index
  • Match and readiness scored separately
  • Tailored résumé with the integrity check visible
  • Assisted apply — we fill, the candidate submits
  • Tracker with the sent-document archive
  • Multi-region profile fields from day one

v1 — make it global

+12 weeks
  • Runtime form discovery: Workday, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters
  • Regional adapters: Naukri, Seek/JobStreet, Xing, Indeed
  • Answer bank reused across employers
  • Targeting variants
  • Letter starters built only from verified facts
  • Follow-ups on applications gone quiet

v2 — autopilot, carefully

After real data
  • Unattended submission, allowlisted destinations only
  • Recruiter-visible profile and inbound interest
  • Interview scheduling handoff
  • Salary and market signal from the index

Out of scope — permanently, not "for now"

  • Solving or outsourcing CAPTCHAs
  • Proxy rotation, fingerprint spoofing, any evasion of a bot wall
  • Writing claims the candidate did not make
  • Auto-answering EEO, consent, health or attestation questions
  • Mass-submitting to employers who have asked automated traffic not to
06

Functional requirements

Profile and résumé
FR-1Upload a résumé — PDF, DOCX, RTF, TXT or MD, up to 8 MB.
FR-2Parse it into structured fields with a confidence score each, presented for correction. Parsing pre-fills; it never silently becomes truth.
FR-3Every parsed field is editable, and the candidate can see the document their profile produces at any time.
FR-4Several résumé files may be stored; exactly one is active per targeting variant.
FR-5Completeness is reported in form fields unlocked, not as a percentage of our own schema.
FR-6Sensitive attributes are never stored, never inferred, never required.
Discovery and matching
FR-7Postings are ingested with source, fetch time and canonical URL recorded.
FR-8Two scores, shown separately and never combined: match and readiness.
FR-9Filters include country, work authorisation, remote policy, language, published salary and posting age.
FR-10A role that is remote in one country only is not shown as an opening to someone who cannot take it.
Preparing an application
FR-11Each posting compiles to a fill plan — ordered actions, each with its source recorded.
FR-12An integrity failure blocks the render rather than degrading it.
FR-13Prose questions get a starter of verified facts plus explicit prompts. Never a finished lie.
FR-14Unresolved fields are surfaced as an answer sheet, in form order, ready to copy.
Tracking
FR-20Every application records status, timestamps, and the hashed bytes of what was sent.
FR-21Re-marking an application never overwrites the first send record.
FR-22Status is submitted, never confirmed, without external evidence.
FR-23Follow-up prompts once an application has been quiet beyond a set window.
Accounts and access
FR-24Email or OAuth sign-up. Browsing needs no account; storing anything does.
FR-25Export everything held about you, machine-readable, without asking a human.
FR-26Delete your account from inside the product — a feature with a visible outcome, not a support ticket.
FR-27Re-authenticate before any Tier B or C run, because that run acts in the candidate's name.
Notifications
FR-28Alerts on saved searches, at a frequency the candidate sets — including never.
FR-29Notify when a run finishes and always when one fails. A silent failure is indistinguishable from a submitted application — the worst state a job seeker can be in.
FR-30Follow-up reminders use the same channel and obey the same frequency setting.
Billing
FR-31Nothing is charged for a run that did not submit. A CAPTCHA is our limit, not the candidate's cost.
FR-32Pricing is purchasing-power adjusted by region. A flat $69.99/month is about a week's wages in several launch markets.
FR-33The free tier completes a real application end to end. A demo tier teaches nobody whether it works.
FR-34Every charge maps to a specific application, visible in the tracker.
Operations
FR-35Destination tiering is configuration, editable without a deploy, every change recorded and attributed.
FR-36Any destination can be disabled instantly and globally by one operator — the response when an employer asks us to stop.
FR-37A published contact address on every automated request, with a human reading the inbox behind it.
FR-38Abuse controls apply to our own users: implausible volume is rate-limited and reviewed, because that behaviour is what gets the platform blocked.

Applying — three tiers, chosen per destination

Tier A
Assisted — the default everywhere FR-15

An extension fills the employer's own form inside the candidate's session; they review and submit. Lawful on every destination, and the only tier permitted where terms prohibit automated access — LinkedIn included.

Tier B
Supervised auto-apply FR-16

Landfall drives the browser, fills everything, and pauses for one confirmation before submitting. Only where that destination's terms permit it.

Tier C
Unattended FR-17 · ships empty

No per-application review. Allowed only for destinations on the submit allowlist, which ships with nothing in it. This is the line between a fast assistant and the thing boards ban.

FR-18 Any CAPTCHA, login wall or rate limit ends the run and hands back the session, with the reason recorded. FR-19 Submission rate is capped per candidate per destination — default 25 a day, adjustable downward only.

07

Non-functional

IDRequirement
NFR-1Identity. Honest User-Agent and contact address on every request. Automated traffic is never dressed as a human browser.
NFR-2Politeness. ~3 requests/second per destination; negative responses cached so 404s are not re-probed.
NFR-3Internationalisation. Non-Latin scripts, right-to-left layout, international phone, address and date formats, and per-country work-authorisation vocabularies — from day one, not as a port.
NFR-4Data residency. EU candidates' data stays in the EU. Region is a deployment property, not a config flag.
NFR-5Accessibility. WCAG 2.2 AA across the candidate-facing app.
NFR-6Auditability. Every automated action reconstructable from logs: what was sent, where, when, on whose instruction.
NFR-7Cost. The 63.6% that needs no model must never quietly start calling one. Budget alarm per application.
NFR-8Security. Résumés and profiles encrypted at rest, secrets in a managed store, a published disclosure route, a penetration test before the first paid user. We hold complete professional identities — credential-grade data, even with no passwords in it.
NFR-9Telemetry. Measure the funnel, not the content. Which fields fail to resolve is a product metric; what a candidate wrote in them is never logged.
NFR-10Availability. 99.5% for the app. Every queued run either completes, fails loudly, or is cancelled — never silently dropped.
NFR-11Verification before live traffic. Executor changes are proved against local fixtures first. Phase 0 ran 565 fill actions across 60 fixtures with zero failures; that harness is the regression gate.

Legal and compliance

The part the competitive set skips. None of this is optional for a platform that says "all over the world".

ConstraintWhat it means for the build
LinkedIn User Agreement §8.2 bans bots and unauthorised automated access; 2025–26 brought ban waves against extensionsLinkedIn is Tier A only — assisted autofill in the user's own session. No bot submission to LinkedIn, ever.
Indeed and Glassdoor forbid robots and scrapersOfficial partner routes or nothing. No scraping.
EU Database Directive protects listings against substantial extractionIngest through APIs and feeds we are permitted to use; never bulk-extract a board's database.
GDPR — we are a controller, often jointly with the candidateExplicit consent before processing a CV; documented lawful basis; access, erasure and portability built as product features, not support tickets.
GDPR Article 9 — CVs can carry health, union or religious dataNever required, never inferred, never used for matching. Flagged and excluded at parse time.
Third parties named in a CV — referees, managersTransparency obligation; we store the document and process no referee data beyond it.
EU AI Act — employer-side hiring AI is high-risk; candidate-side tools are not restricted by itWe are candidate-side. Say so plainly, and never market as a hiring-decision system.
India DPDP Act · CCPASame rights surface, region-aware retention.
Fraud — knowingly false statements in an applicationThe honesty principles are the compliance control. A platform that writes claims for a user manufactures this risk; we architecturally cannot.

Retention, erasure, and a real conflict inside this document

FR-20 says every send is archived and never overwritten. FR-25 and FR-26 say a candidate can take their data and erase their account. Both are correct and they collide — so the resolution belongs here, not in a bug report six months from now.

Erasure wins

Deleting an account deletes the archives with it. The archive serves the candidate's own memory of what they sent; it is not a compliance record we are obliged to keep, and there is no lawful basis for holding a CV after consent is withdrawn.

Immutability holds while the account lives

"Written once" is a rule about our code never rewriting history — not a claim that the candidate cannot end it.

Default retention: 24 months

From the send, then automatic deletion, shortenable by the candidate. An application older than two years is history, not a live thread.

Position

Landfall's default is assisted, not unattended, because the default destination's terms say so. Volume comes from removing the typing, not from removing the human.

Architecture — static front end, sandboxed executor

ingest/     ATS and board adapters · schema probes · live index
profile/    structured facts · variants · résumé files · parse + confidence
plan/       deterministic fill-plan compiler — no browser, no model
compose/    bullet selection · integrity check · starters (thin model edge)
execute/    Tier A extension · Tier B sandboxed browser · Tier C allowlisted
record/     tracker · sent-bytes archive · hashes · follow-ups
trust/      consent · retention · erasure · region routing · rate limits

Phase 0's TypeScript adapters, schema probes and fill-plan compiler port straight into ingest/ and plan/ — roughly a third of v0 already built and measured.

The front end is static, and that is a decision

The candidate-facing app ships as static assets behind a CDN, talking to a regional API. No server-side rendering.

It is a tool, not a content site

SEO is irrelevant behind a login, so rendering on the server buys nothing and costs a tier. Marketing pages are a separate static site, indexed properly.

Data residency gets easier

Assets are global; API and database are regional. An EU candidate's data never leaves the EU because their API origin never does.

The cost, stated honestly

Search and the index must be real API endpoints rather than rendered pages, and first paint needs a genuinely useful empty state instead of a spinner.

The executor is sandboxed, per session, and disposable

Anything that drives a browser runs in a one-shot container: created for a single application run, destroyed after it. No shared cookie jar, no shared profile, no browser state reused between candidates.

Tier A — assistedTier B — supervisedTier C — unattended
Runs inThe candidate's own browser, via extensionSandboxed container, one per runSandboxed container, one per run
Network identityTheir IP, their sessionRegional egress, honest UA, rate-limitedSame, plus allowlist
Candidate seesTheir own form, filledLive view, one confirm before submitNothing live; full recording after
CredentialsNever leave their machineInjected per run, never persistedInjected per run, never persisted
Allowed whereEverywhereWhere terms permitAllowlist only — ships empty
Why sandboxing is a requirement, not an optimisation

Credential containment. Tier B may hold an ATS login for the length of one run; a long-lived shared profile would pool candidates' sessions in one process — one bug away from a cross-candidate leak.

Hostile-page containment. We load employer pages we do not control, running their scripts. That belongs in a disposable container, not next to the API.

Honest network identity. One egress per region with a truthful User-Agent is a fixed, inspectable footprint. A rotating residential proxy pool is what an evasion product looks like — and principle 3 rules it out anyway.

Failure isolation. A hung browser kills its own container and one application, never a queue.

60–180sOne vCPU and ~1.5 GB per application run
~$0.005Compute per application — not the constraint
per ATSRuntime form discovery must be written and maintained separately — this is the real v1 budget line
63.6%Of every form resolves before a container is ever started

The plan compiler, matching, tailoring and the integrity check stay outside the sandbox entirely — pure functions over stored facts, no browser, no network. That is why most applications never need a container at all.

13

Decisions needed before build starts

01

Business model

Free tier plus subscription, or credits charged only on successful submission? LiftmyCV sits at $9.99–$69.99 and charges nothing when a CAPTCHA blocks it. This shapes the schema and the whole execute path.

02

Launch region

India-first — which makes Naukri a v0 integration rather than v1 — or global-English-first on Greenhouse, as scoped above?

03

Tier C at all

Recommendation: build the allowlist mechanism in v0, ship it empty, and decide with real data rather than in advance.

04

Résumé parsing — build or buy

Buying an extraction API is faster and puts candidate CVs through a third party, which section 8 then has to account for.

14

Technology

TypeScript on both ends. Not a preference — close to forced by what already exists.

LayerChoiceWhy this one
Front endTypeScript · React · ViteStatic build to a CDN, which section 9 commits to. Familiar from guidetrack, so the concepts port
APITypeScript on Node · Fastify or HonoPhase 0's adapters, schema probes, plan compiler and integrity check are already TypeScript
ExecutorNode · PlaywrightPlaywright's first-class language is Node, and the 60-fixture harness is already written against it

Queuepg-bossOne datastore rather than two, until volume argues otherwise

The decisive argument

Rewriting Phase 0 in Python or Go discards the only part of this product that has been measured — 541 postings, 565 fill actions, zero failures. The types in plan/ and ingest/ are the specification. Porting them means re-deriving that for no gain.

One honest exception

If decision 4 goes toward building résumé parsing rather than buying it, Python wins on merit — pdfplumber, spaCy and the extraction ecosystem live there. That is one service behind an HTTP boundary, not a second language through the codebase.

Deliberately not used

Next.js. Section 9 rules out server-side rendering, so running it purely for a static export carries a render framework we have decided not to use. Vite does that job with less.

Hosting shape, per region

Data residency (NFR-4) is the constraint that decides the topology.

TierDeploymentNotes
Static assetsOne global CDNThey carry no candidate data, so there is no residency question
API + PostgresPer region — EU, US, IndiaA candidate's origin is regional, so their data never crosses a border it should not
Executor containersPer region, pooled, one-shotRegional egress gives each region one honest, inspectable IP footprint (NFR-1)
Object storagePer regionRésumé files and send archives live beside their region's database

Adding a country means adding a region, not re-architecting. That is the whole reason for keeping rendering out of the server tier.
